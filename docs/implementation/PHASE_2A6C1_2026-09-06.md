**Started:** 2026-09-05  **Completed:** 2026-09-06

## Why this package exists

Package 2A.6C executed the rebuild under the owner's Collection-1 review and
stopped at the accepted extent-coverage floor: months 01–04 reached 0.4459,
0.8325, 0.9710 and 0.9460 against `>= 0.99`. Coverage was the *only* failure.

Measurement of every reachable alternative established that no single source
policy fixes it:

| Option | January coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Collection-1, cloud < 40 (as executed) | 0.4467 | fails |
| cloud < 60, Collection-1 | 0.7520 | fails |
| unfiltered cloud, Collection-1 | 0.8314 | fails |
| + quiet-ENSO years 2018/2020 | 0.4469 | fails |
| + El Niño years 2023/2024 | 0.9143 | fails, and distorts the normal |
| widen the registry for the whole year | 0.9965 | clears, but 59.3% of January still below three contributions |

The cause is the state of the ESA archive, not a configuration choice: in
months 1–4 of the accepted source years the Collection-1 lineage exists almost
only for 2023–2025 (2 to 6 datatakes per year for 2016–2022, against 48, 49
and 56 for 2023, 2024 and 2025).

The owner accepted a **wet-season source regime** on 2026-09-05: widen the
registry for months 1–4 only, relax that regime's cloud filter, and declare the
wet season explicitly because the compromise is one of reproducibility.

## The contract change

Mapped in `docs/implementation/PHASE_2A6C1_SEASONAL_REGIME_MAP.md`. The
load-bearing finding was what did **not** need to change: `reviewed_baselines`
was already a parameter of every closed-science entry point, so
`scl_mask_v2`, `composition_v2`, `gee_composition_v2` and `identity_v3` are
byte-unchanged and the accepted science is untouched.

A **source regime** owns a disjoint set of calendar months and carries its own
reviewed-baseline registry, scene cloud filter and declared provenance state.

| Regime | Months | Cloud | Registry | Provenance state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wet-season-mixed-lineage-v1` | 1–4 | < 60 | 05.xx **+ 02.11, 02.14, 03.00, 03.01, 04.00** | `mixed_lineage_pending_esa_reprocessing` |
| `dry-season-collection1-v1` | 5–12 | < 40 | 05.xx only | `collection1_lineage_stable` |

Regimes must partition months 1–12 exactly: a gap would leave a month with no
source policy and an overlap would give it two, so both fail closed. A scene is
admitted only by the registry of the month it belongs to; the manifest-level
registry is the union of what is admitted *somewhere* and is never the test.

**The coverage floor was not lowered.** The accepted policy clears 0.99 in
every month, so Package 2A.2 discipline is kept rather than relaxed to fit.

Artifacts: `config/phase2a6c1_seasonal_source_regime_amendment_v1.json` and
`config/phase2a6c1_wet_season_processing_baseline_review_v1.json`, both
checksum-bound into the plan and the manifest. The 2A.6C review stays
byte-unchanged and applies to both regimes. The wet review admits exactly the
five values observed in months 1–4 under the regime filter; `02.12` and `02.13`
never occur there and stay fail-closed in both regimes.

The cloud filter is 60, not 80 or unfiltered, on measured evidence: a
cloud-leakage test found no systematic contamination in NDMI or NBR — the sign
was inconsistent and reversed in March — but EVI2, the only index using the red
band that thin cloud brightens most, carries a positive offset growing
monotonically with cloud, `+0.027` at 40–60% and `+0.043` at 60–80% against a
clean January dispersion of `0.087`. The test covered 12–25% of the extent in
January and 74.5% in March, spanned two months, and did not fully separate
contamination from the correlation between cloud and rainfall. It is recorded
as partial evidence, not a clearance.

## Open scientific question — it moved

The wet-season regime worked well enough that **the shallowest months are now
May (8.86M pixels below three contributions) and December (6.32M)**, both in
the dry regime, followed by January (11.58M, still the worst). These are the
shoulder months of the dry season, where the Collection-1 restriction bites for
the same archive reason and no regime currently compensates.

No minimum contribution depth is imposed. Choosing one remains an owner and
Phase 5 decision, and the manifest measures and reports per index and month.
Whether the shoulder months deserve their own regime is a question this
evidence raises and does not answer.

## Retirement condition

The wet regime declares `mixed_lineage_pending_esa_reprocessing`. ESA is
actively reprocessing these very months — January 2019 stood at 7 of 25 scenes,
April 2019 at 1 of 14, while August 2021 was already at 65 of 78 — so the
admitted wet-season products are precisely those not yet reached. When the
reprocessing arrives, the wet season should be rebuilt on the Collection-1
lineage and the regime retired. The state is machine-readable so that
retirement has a precise target.

## Addendum 2026-09-06 — the shoulder regime, and baseline 2.1.0

Baseline `2.0.0` left May and December as the shallowest months of the year:
16.5% and 11.7% of the grid below three contributions, against 0.0% for
July–November. The owner accepted a third regime for them.

### Why a looser filter and not a wider registry

Measured before deciding, for May and December:

| Option | May below-three | December below-three | Reproducibility cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collection-1, cloud < 40 (as in 2.0.0) | 16.5% | 11.8% | none |
| **Collection-1, cloud < 60** | **3.5%** | **1.6%** | **none** |
| widened registry, cloud < 40 | 0.0% | 0.3% | loses the Collection-1 lineage |
| widened registry, cloud < 60 | 0.0% | 0.0% | loses the Collection-1 lineage |

The shoulder months need only the looser filter. Widening the registry would
have bought a further three points at the price of the lineage — the same
reproducibility compromise the wet season had to accept — for months that do
not need it. Rejected.

Months 6–11 keep the accepted filter of 40: they already sit at 0.0%, so a
looser filter would buy them nothing while taking on the measured EVI2 cloud
bias.

A check that mattered: the executor rejects a *whole datatake* that mixes
admitted and rejected baselines, while the scenario measurement filtered at
scene level. At cloud < 60 in months 5 and 12 there are **zero** mixed
datatakes, so the measured 28/28 passes survive the stricter rule exactly.

### Why the version changed

Two months of pixels differ, so this is baseline **2.1.0**. A result computed
against "the v2 baseline" has to mean exactly one thing; leaving the label at
2.0.0 while the content changed is precisely the irreproducibility the 2A.2
audit recorded against 1.0.0. The version, the object key prefix
(`baselines_v2/2.1.0/`), the local directory and the manifest path all moved.
`config/baseline_manifest_v2.json` stays as the immutable 2.0.0 record and
`config/phase2a6c1_seasonal_source_regime_amendment_v1.json` as the amendment
that produced it.
